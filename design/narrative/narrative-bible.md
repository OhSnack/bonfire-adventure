# Narrative Bible: Bonefire

*Spoilers. Story canon for writers and the narrative team. Decided 2026-11-01 unless
marked Proposed or Open. The concept summary is in `design/gdd/game-concept.md`
(Narrative Premise); the visual limits on dog clues are in `design/art/art-bible.md`,
Section 1. Names (Ashmedai, the Angel of Death, the Keeper) are placeholders.*

## Premise and memory rules

- **The twist** (Decided): the hero is not the child but the child's dog, reshaped in
  the crossing into the child's likeness. Scent is a dog trait, which is why it is
  shown only as spirit wisps.
- **The truth.** The Angel of Death comes for the boy. His dog sees it and follows
  the Angel into the spirit world to save him. The boy's soul is with the Angel for
  the whole run; reaching the Angel is reaching the boy.
- **The crossing.** Stripped of form and memory in the crossing, the dog becomes a
  childlike skeleton. All he keeps is that he wanted to save someone, and that stays
  true to the end.
- **The reversed memories.** Each recovered memory is the dog's own memory of a real
  moment shared with the boy. It is narrated with first and third person swapped:
  what the boy did is told as "I", and what the dog did is told as "{dog's name}".
  The hero therefore speaks as the boy. Because the events are real, the colour each
  memory returns (art bible Section 1) is earned. The one exception is the crossing
  night, below.
- **The crossing night.** The one memory with no swap: "I" really is the dog, and
  every clause is true except the single line Ashmedai planted.
  - *Opening of the game* (fragment 1, true, deliberately blurry): "We were sleeping
    in the room, and then an entity came, I cannot remember what it was... I felt
    cold... The entire room got silent, and something sour... spoiled... so strong I
    got dizzy. The being... It took something, I cannot remember what... But I
    remember I followed it through the door it left open."
  - *First region 2 clear* (the planted lie): "It took my best friend. It took
    {dog's name}." This is Ashmedai's only edit: he changed who was taken and nothing
    else. "My best friend" stays true both ways.
  - *Door quest* (Proposed outline): later fragments add true detail that strains
    against the lie without contradicting it, e.g. "It bent over the bed. It was
    gentle, like it was sorry" and "The bed was warm, and then it wasn't."
  - The sourness is Ashmedai's mark, not the Angel's smell; it returns at the dark
    pools. The cold and the silence are the Angel's, who stays solemn, not monstrous.
- **Why they are reversed.** Ashmedai (placeholder name), a chained being of chaos,
  noticed a foreign soul entering the realm and scrambled him in the crossing, and
  also corrupted the Keeper of memories' view. The hero never chose this; there is no
  death wish. Who chained Ashmedai is Open.
- **Ashmedai in play.** He is glimpsed through dark pools, where he offers Chaos
  Nerlings and upgrades: he is the only way into the Chaos family. He first appears
  in the first room of the hero's first visit to region 3, after the planted lie;
  from then on dark pools can appear in any run. The memories never name the Angel,
  so he names it for the hero and offers help against their "common enemy". Every
  offer shows its cost before the hero accepts. After a few meetings and favours, a
  dark pool at the haven lets the hero contact him. His motives stay unknown: the
  Dark Bargain sides the hero with him against the Angel, then he turns on the hero,
  and only defeating him as an alternative boss reveals that he scrambled the
  memories to force the Bargain and overthrow the Angel. The Bargain, the betrayal
  and that boss are reachable only after the first Angel dialogue, so they cannot
  leak the twist.
- **The holes.** As memories assemble, the story stops fitting: one hole per memory
  chapter, in dialogue or memory-card text, never in combat or the hero's silhouette,
  tied to the order memories unlock. Hole text shows when a memory card is gained and
  in the memory log, never on the card face in combat. The pronoun swap
  is the main device: the narration knows things only the dog could know and tells
  them about "{dog's name}". Each hole must pass a second-read check. Holes and dog
  clues are separate budgets; dog-clue limits are in art bible Section 1.
- **The locked door.** The Angel's door is locked. The first run that reaches it ends
  in a dialogue at the door (not the reveal) and a "win?" run-end screen instead of
  defeat. It pays Embers like a clear once per profile; later runs that reach the
  locked door pay the normal rate. A quest whose steps are memory chapters opens the
  door, so the reveal always comes after several holes. Difficulty tiers unlock on
  the first full clear (owner decision, 2026-11-01).
- **The reveal.** At the first dialogue with the Angel of Death, past the opened
  door, the hero understands
  his memories were reversed: it was never the dog that was dying. The alternative
  ending key cards (Forgiveness, Dark Bargain) cannot be earned before that first
  Angel dialogue, so no ending is ever chosen without the truth.
- **Persistence.** Memories, bonds and story progress are kept across failed runs.
- **Bonds** grow through NPC quests and repeat runs, never through Embers.
  *Proposed:* a hunting quest aimed at Ashmedai once the player knows who he is; it
  must be recast now that he is chained and fought as a boss (content creation).

## Open

- Where the door-quest fragments of the crossing night unlock.
